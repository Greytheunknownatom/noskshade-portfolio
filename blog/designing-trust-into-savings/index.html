Nobody told me the hardest part of designing a savings app would be fear

I thought I was designing for people who wanted to save money. I was really designing for people who had already lost it.

Philip Nosike · 4 November 2026 · 6 min read

Ten minutes into a research call, a woman stopped answering my questions and started telling me a story.

Years ago she’d joined a savings group with people she trusted. One day the person holding the money stopped picking up calls.

“So,” she said, “why would I trust an app?”

I didn’t have an answer. That was the moment the KOYNX project changed for me.

People had heard every promise already

Almost everyone we spoke to had a version of that story. A group that collapsed. An app that suddenly froze withdrawals. A friend who “would pay back next month.”

Big words like “secure” and “guaranteed” didn’t calm anyone down. They’d heard all of that before.

Trust isn’t something you say. It’s something people can check, again and again, without asking you.

So we stopped promising and started showing

  • Put the boring details right where people look: when the money unlocks, and what happens if you need it early.
  • Let group members see who has paid this round, so nobody has to chase anyone.
  • Show the name on the receiving account before the PIN, so there’s no doubt about where money is going.

None of these were exciting to design. All of them came straight from people’s fears.

What I took away

Next time you start a fintech project, ask people about money they’ve lost, not just money they want to save.

Their answers will show you exactly which screens need to work hardest.
