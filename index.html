Case Study · Higher Education · 2026

HillCross College

A homepage hero revamp for the January 2027/2028 admissions intake, designed to tell prospective students that applications are open and move them straight into an application.

Role
Product Designer
Scope
Hero, announcement bar, nav
Platform
Web, responsive
Goal
Applications for the new intake
hillcross.ac.za
01 · The brief

An intake campaign needed a front door

Each year HillCross opens applications for a new intake. The homepage hero was the first thing prospective students saw, but it was written as a general welcome: a long institutional paragraph, one apply button and a cut-out stock photo. Nothing on the page said that admissions were open, or for which year.

The brief was to rebuild the hero around the January 2027/2028 intake and keep the credibility markers the college already had.

02 · Before and after

From a welcome to an invitation

Before

General welcome headline, a 63-word institutional paragraph, one CTA and the stats tucked bottom right.

After

Intake announcement bar, intake-led headline, two calls to action, campus photography and slider controls.

03 · What changed

Five decisions behind the new hero

01

An announcement bar for the intake

A full-width red bar states the intake in plain words and carries its own Apply Now button. It sits above the navigation on every page, so a student who lands deep in the site still sees that admissions are open.

02

A headline about the student

"Welcome to Hillcross College" became "Your Next Chapter Starts this Intake." The orange highlight on Intake ties the headline to the campaign, and the 63-word institutional paragraph became one line naming the academic year.

03

Two clear next steps

"Start your application" is the primary action. "Entry requirements" sits beside it as the secondary path for students who want to check eligibility before committing.

04

Proof at the moment of decision

The 3000+ graduates, 90% employment rate and 24+ departments moved from the bottom right into a glass panel under the calls to action, where they support the decision to apply.

05

Real campus photography

The cut-out stock group was replaced with students photographed on campus. A dark scrim on the left keeps the white headline readable over the photo.

04 · Why a slider

Why the hero became a slider

One campaign, more than one story
The intake message leads on the first slide. Later slides can carry faculties, campus life or open days without crowding a single frame.
Swappable each season
When the intake closes, only the campaign slide changes. The rest of the hero stays, so there is no redesign every January.
The visitor stays in control
Previous and next buttons, a 02 / 02 counter, a progress bar and a pause button mean the slider never moves content away from someone who is still reading.
Part of the campaign palette
The yellow progress bar and white controls pick up the announcement bar colours, so the slider reads as part of the admissions campaign.

Built to store things, not to guide teachers

The old teacher interface listed tools and records, but gave no sense of what mattered today. Grading, lessons and messages lived in separate places, and there was no view of how students were engaging.

The goal: one overview that answers three questions on sign-in. How is my class doing? What do I need to do? What has changed?

Building and maintaining it while shipping

The teacher dashboard was designed on the same system as the student portal, extended as new screens needed new parts and maintained so the three portals (students, teachers and admin) stay consistent.

Components reused

Metric cards, tab bars with counts, status chips, progress bars, feed items and empty states come from one library.

New parts for teachers

The engagement panel, grading queue cards and lesson completion rows were added to the library, then reused by admin.

States designed once

Empty, in progress and filled versions of each card, so a new account never looks broken.

Case Study · Fintech · Mobile app

BlissBill

Airtime, data, electricity, cable, betting, exam PINs and a virtual card, in one Nigerian bill payment app that talks like its users.

Product DesignerMobile appNigeriaLight + dark mode
01 · The problem

Paying small bills meant juggling apps, USSD codes and agents

A typical week means airtime, a data bundle, an electricity token and a TV subscription, each bought somewhere different. BlissBill puts them behind one balance, for people who top up often and in small amounts.

Who it’s for: young Nigerians who pay for everyday services from their phone and want it to feel quick, safe and familiar.

02 · Research & insights

What shaped the design

Bills are many and small

Users pay several low-value bills a week, so the most used services need to be one tap from home.

Trust comes before money

A wallet needs NIN, BVN and a selfie check. The steps have to feel guided, not like a form.

Tone builds familiarity

Short Pidgin phrases ("Oya enter", "You sabi!") make a finance app feel local and friendly.

Phones are used day and night

Many sessions happen late, on low brightness. A full dark mode was part of the scope from the start.

03 · Before → after

From a flat first concept to a warmer redesign

Concept v1

A solid yellow header, dark tiles and features framed as marketing posters.

Redesign

A patterned balance card, grouped services, a promo slot and illustrated people.

04 · Key decisions

Reading the home screen

1234
1
Balance first

"All your money!" sits on a patterned card with Add Money on it and a toggle to hide the amount.

2
Six services up front

Airtime, Data, Cable, Electricity, Book flights and WAEC, with See more for the full list.

3
Room for offers

A wide promo banner in the brand’s teal and gold, shown between services and extras.

4
Four-tab navigation

Home, Wallet, History and Me, with the active tab in gold.

Sign-up

Three steps, one question at a time

Phone number, then name and date of birth, then password and PIN. Each screen shows "Step 1 of 3" and keeps Next disabled until the step is complete.

Identity check

A selfie check that explains itself

Clear instructions before the camera opens, an illustrated upload state, then the photo with Proceed and Retake.

Virtual card

A card that carries the brand

The Prime virtual card uses the same pattern and illustration as the app, with Visa or Mastercard as a choice.

Case Study · On-demand service · Mobile app

Laundromate

Laundry collected from your door and delivered back clean, booked in one flow from price check to receipt, for customers across Africa.

Product DesignerMobile appAfrica-wide8 flows · 45 screens
01 · The problem

Laundry eats the weekend

Washing, ironing and getting bulky items like duvets cleaned takes hours many busy people don’t have. Laundry services exist, but prices are unclear until you call, and pickup times are hard to pin down.

Who it’s for: busy professionals, families and students in cities across Africa, from Nairobi in Kenya to Abuja in Nigeria, who would rather book a pickup than spend a day on laundry.

02 · Research & insights

What shaped the design

Price is the first question

People want to know the cost before they commit, in their own currency and for their own city.

Timing has to fit around work

Collection and delivery need their own day and time window, and regular customers want a repeat schedule.

Orders are mixed

One bag often holds washing, ironing and a duvet, so services must stack in a single order.

No surprises at checkout

Every fee, offer and tip should be visible before paying, and the receipt should match it.
