Fintech · Mobile app

BlissBill

Airtime, data, electricity, cable, betting, exam PINs and a virtual card, in one Nigerian bill payment app that talks like its users.

The result

Seven flows designed end to end in light and dark mode, from sign-up and identity checks to bill payments and a virtual card.

What I did

I took the flat first concept and redesigned it: home, sign-up, NIN, BVN and selfie checks, bill flows, the virtual card, and a full dark mode built on the same tokens.

The problem

Paying small, frequent bills meant juggling apps, USSD codes and agents.

Paying small bills meant juggling apps, USSD codes and agents

A typical week means airtime, a data bundle, an electricity token and a TV subscription, each bought somewhere different. BlissBill puts them behind one balance, for people who top up often and in small amounts.

Who it’s for: young Nigerians who pay for everyday services from their phone and want it to feel quick, safe and familiar.

What shaped the design

Bills are many and small

Users pay several low-value bills a week, so the most used services need to be one tap from home.

Trust comes before money

A wallet needs NIN, BVN and a selfie check. The steps have to feel guided, not like a form.

Tone builds familiarity

Short Pidgin phrases ("Oya enter", "You sabi!") make a finance app feel local and friendly.

Phones are used day and night

Many sessions happen late, on low brightness. A full dark mode was part of the scope from the start.

Reading the home screen

"All your money!" sits on a patterned card with Add Money on it and a toggle to hide the amount.

Airtime, Data, Cable, Electricity, Book flights and WAEC, with See more for the full list.

A wide promo banner in the brand’s teal and gold, shown between services and extras.

Home, Wallet, History and Me, with the active tab in gold.

Three steps, one question at a time

Phone number, then name and date of birth, then password and PIN. Each screen shows "Step 1 of 3" and keeps Next disabled until the step is complete.

A selfie check that explains itself

Clear instructions before the camera opens, an illustrated upload state, then the photo with Proceed and Retake.

A card that carries the brand

The Prime virtual card uses the same pattern and illustration as the app, with Visa or Mastercard as a choice.

One app, two moods

The gold pattern, illustrations and service colours stay the same in both themes. Only surfaces and text switch, so every screen reads as BlissBill.

What bill payers told us

People who buy airtime and data and pay bills tried both versions of the app.

“Airtime and data are on the home screen, so I can top up in a few taps.”
“I check the meter details twice before paying. Showing them before I confirm makes me relax.”
“I pay most bills at night. Dark mode is easier on my eyes.”

The parts every screen is built from

Titles and buttons in bold, body and inputs in regular, with Catamaran for longer headings on sign-up.

What shipped

The redesign delivered a complete mobile UI: onboarding, sign-up with identity checks, the home and wallet, bill payment flows and the virtual card, in both themes and built on one set of colours, type and components.

A gold-on-pattern identity, light and dark

BlissBill’s guide pairs a warm gold with bold African patterns, and every component was defined for both light and dark mode.

The trade-off

A full dark mode doubled the states to design and maintain. Most sessions happen at night, so it was in scope from day one.

What's next

I'd test whether the Pidgin microcopy still feels friendly when something goes wrong, such as a failed payment.
