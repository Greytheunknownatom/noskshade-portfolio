HeatBud

A small heat buddy for Nigeria. It tells you how hot it really feels, when it’s safest to go out, and how much water you need today, in plain words, before the sun catches you off guard.

The result

Live and installable at heatbud.netlify.app: feels-like temperature, a seven-day heat forecast, a personal water plan and three ways to be reminded.

What I did

I designed, built and shipped it myself, from the research and UI to the PWA, push alerts and deployment.

The problem

Weather apps show a number, not what it means for your day: when to go out and how much to drink.

Opens on any phone. Add it to your home screen to use it like an app.

El Niño was on the news. I was too busy to notice the heat.

Every week there was more talk about El Niño and hotter, drier months. Meanwhile my own days were full of back-to-back work. I’d look up in the afternoon with a headache, realise I’d barely had water, and only then notice how hot it was.

Weather apps gave me a number. None of them told me what that number meant for my day: when to step out, when to stay in, and how much to drink.

And I work indoors. Millions of Nigerians spend their whole day under the sun.

Built for me first, shaped for anyone out in the sun

The busy desk worker

Forgets to drink until the headache arrives. Needs a nudge, not a lecture.

People who work outside

Riders, traders, builders, delivery staff. They can’t avoid the sun, but they can plan breaks and water around it.

Students and commuters

Long walks, bus stops and hot classrooms. Knowing the safest hour to move makes a real difference.

Families looking out for others

Parents and carers keeping an eye on children and older relatives, who feel heat faster.

A weather number is data. HeatBud turns it into a decision.

Feels like comes first

Air temperature hides humidity. The big number on screen is what your body actually feels.

Say what to do

“OK with precautions until 11 AM. Carry water, wear a hat.” One sentence you can act on.

Make it feel local

An illustrated Abuja scene, Nigerian time and plain English, so it feels like it was made here.

Friendly, never scary

A buddy character and warm colours keep you coming back, without turning heat into panic.

Five tabs, one job: get you through the day safely

Everything you need in one glance

Feels-like temperature, a colour-coded rating, humidity, UV, wind, rain and air quality, with one plain sentence that tells you what to do right now. Below it sit a heat dial, the day’s safest window and your water bottle.

Plan around the heat, not into it

A seven-day view and an hour-by-hour strip coloured Safe, Caution, High risk or Dangerous. The best time out is pinned at the top, with sunrise, sunset, peak UV and your water target for the day.

A daily water plan that follows the heat

Your target comes from your weight, how active you are and the day’s forecast. It’s spread across your waking hours, with more during the hottest stretch marked “do not skip”, plus simple advice for heading outdoors.

Reminders that work, and a buddy that’s yours

Push alerts, in-app reminders and calendar alarms that keep working even when the app is closed. In Me you pick one of twelve buddies, set your body and routine, change location, and add a doctor’s fluid limit if you have one.

What was harder than it looked

Showing water without taking over

My first version filled the whole Water page like a glass. It looked fun, but it buried everything else. I moved tracking onto Home as a real bottle with a side scale that fills as you drink, and kept the Water tab for the plan and schedule.

Getting reminders to actually arrive

Push notifications on the web need a server and the right permissions on every phone. Dragging files onto Netlify broke the server side, so I switched to deploying from the command line. I also added calendar alarms as a backup that works with no server at all.

Being helpful without being unsafe

More water isn’t right for everyone. People with heart, kidney or liver conditions can be harmed by it. HeatBud asks about this, keeps targets inside a doctor’s limit, and includes a guide to warning signs.

Making the city look like home

The illustrated Abuja scene only works if people recognise it. Getting landmarks to look accurate and real, not generic, is still a work in progress.

Live, installable and in my pocket

I designed it, built it and shipped it myself. It’s also my first real step from product designer to design engineer.
