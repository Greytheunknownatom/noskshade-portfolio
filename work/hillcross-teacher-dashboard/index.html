HillCross College Teacher Dashboard

A revamp of the legacy teacher portal into one overview that shows a lecturer how their class is doing, what needs grading and what is new, the moment they sign in.

The result

Signing in now answers three questions on one screen: how the class is doing, what needs grading and what has changed.

What I did

I redesigned the legacy teacher portal: the overview, the engagement panel, the grading queue, lesson completion rows and empty states. All of it was added to the shared HillCross library.

The problem

The old portal stored records but gave lecturers no sense of what mattered today.

Built to store things, not to guide teachers

The old teacher interface listed tools and records, but gave no sense of what mattered today. Grading, lessons and messages lived in separate places, and there was no view of how students were engaging.

The goal: one overview that answers three questions on sign-in. How is my class doing? What do I need to do? What has changed?

Everything a teacher needs, in the order they need it

Online students, students active now, pending grading with its next deadline, and video completion rate, each with this month’s change.

A 7-day engagement score with highly active, moderately active and inactive students, so a teacher can see who is falling behind.

Overview, Lessons, Assignments, Assessment, Examination, Result and Messages, each with a count of what’s new.

A mandatory 15-minute classroom guide and a live feed of recent activity and announcements.

Pending work filtered by assignments, assessments and exams, with submissions counted and a Begin Grading action.

Each lesson’s watch length and average student completion rate.

Designed for an empty account and a busy one

Zeros instead of blank cards, a completed orientation in the feed, and illustrated empty states that point to the next step: Create Course Activities and Upload Lessons.

The same layout filled with real work: 16 items to grade, 67.2% engagement, announcements about exams and staff meetings, and lessons with completion rates.

What lecturers told us

Lecturers walked through grading, lessons and announcements on the new overview.

“I can see what still needs marking the moment I log in, instead of opening every class.”
“Knowing which class is falling behind this week is the thing I actually need.”
“Posting one announcement to all my classes at once saves me a lot of copying and pasting.”

Building and maintaining it while shipping

The teacher dashboard was designed on the same system as the student portal, extended as new screens needed new parts and maintained so the three portals (students, teachers and admin) stay consistent.

Shared foundations

HillCross red, neutral greys, one type scale and spacing grid across all three portals.

Components reused

Metric cards, tab bars with counts, status chips, progress bars, feed items and empty states come from one library.

New parts for teachers

The engagement panel, grading queue cards and lesson completion rows were added to the library, then reused by admin.

States designed once

Empty, in progress and filled versions of each card, so a new account never looks broken.

The trade-off

Showing zeros and empty states instead of hiding cards makes a new account look sparse. In return the layout never changes, so a teacher learns it once.

What's next

The engagement panel and grading cards are already reused in the admin portal. Next is measuring how quickly pending work gets graded once teachers can see the queue.
